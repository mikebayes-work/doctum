--- title: Fully funded, competition funded or self-funded? What PhD funding labels mean date: 2026-10-31 tag: Funding excerpt: PhD adverts use funding labels that sound similar but mean very different things. Here's what each one usually means and what to check before you apply. ---

Two PhD adverts can look almost identical and still be worlds apart on money. One pays your fees and a stipend to live on. The other expects you to find tens of thousands of pounds yourself. The difference is usually one phrase in the funding section. Here's how to read it.

Fully funded

A fully funded PhD normally pays your tuition fees and a tax-free stipend for living costs for the length of the project, often three and a half to four years. Many are funded by UK Research and Innovation (UKRI) councils, a university, a charity or an industry partner.

The question to check: does it cover international fees? Some fully funded places pay only the Home fee rate. An international student offered one of these may have to make up the difference, which can be large. If the advert doesn't say, ask the supervisor before you apply.

Competition funded

Competition funded means funding exists, but there are more projects than awards. You apply for the project, and if you're shortlisted you compete with candidates from other projects for a limited number of studentships. Doctoral training partnerships (DTPs) and centres for doctoral training (CDTs) often work this way.

These are worth applying for, but a strong application matters more than usual. Contact the supervisor early and treat the interview as a competition, not a formality.

Partially funded

Some of the cost is covered, for example fees only, or a stipend below the usual rate. Work out exactly what you would still need to pay before you apply.

Self-funded

You pay tuition fees and your living costs, or bring your own scholarship, sponsor or government funding. Many self-funded adverts are open to anyone who can fund themselves, which is why they are common for international applicants with a scholarship from their home country.

What to check on every advert

  • Which fees are covered: Home only, or international too?
  • The stipend amount, and whether it rises each year.
  • How long the funding lasts compared with the length of the PhD.
  • Whether the funding is confirmed or still competitive.
  • Any eligibility rules: residency, prior degree class or a Master's.
